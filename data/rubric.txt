PART 2: PATTERNS IN HIGH-RATED HIRES THAT A JOB SPEC WOULD NOT ASK FOR
======================================================================

PATTERN 1: "Ops first, function second"  (5 of 5 high-rated, 0 of 3 others)
Every Exceeds hire did hands-on freight/logistics operations work BEFORE
moving into the function Kargo hired them for.
  - Rohan: operations executive at a CHA firm at JNPT (180+ shipments/month)
    before becoming an engineer.
  - Sunita: documentation & compliance executive (200+ shipments/month)
    before operations consulting.
  - Aditya: port services sales at JNPT, worked alongside terminal ops
    during peak periods, before SaaS sales.
  - Meghna: customer relations & documentation at a freight forwarder
    before SaaS customer success.
  - Lavanya: carrier allocation and exception management at a 3PL before PM.
None of the comparison group has this. Vikram (HR tech), Rahul (fintech /
HR tech) and Preetham (e-commerce; touched logistics only via API
integrations) came in with the function but not the floor.
This is the strongest signal in the data. A PM spec lists "domain
knowledge" at best; it does not ask "have you done the customer's job?"

PATTERN 2: "Saw the pain, built the fix, others adopted it without a mandate"
The high-rated hires build unasked-for tools from problems they personally
hit, and those tools spread to peers.
  - Rohan: Excel shipment tracker adopted by a 12-person ops team in two
    weeks; BoL verification prototype built over a weekend, 30 users in a
    month.
  - Sunita: rebuilt the documentation intake workflow over a weekend when
    a vendor changed format; kept permanently by the team.
  - Lavanya: Excel visibility dashboard -> Tableau, adopted by 2 other
    regional teams; first-ever carrier scorecard process at her branch.
  - Meghna: 30-60-90 onboarding framework now used by the whole CS team.
Note: the comparison group shows some initiative too (Vikram's PRD
template, Rahul's case-study programme), but it sits inside their function
rather than originating from operational pain. Weaker discriminator than
Pattern 1, so it is weighted lower.

PATTERN 3: "No layer between them and the outcome", plus candour about failure
High-rated hires have repeatedly worked where nobody sat between them and
the consequence, and they write openly about what did not work.
  - Lavanya: sole PM for three product areas; killed 2 of her own features
    on usage data; wrote the outage post-mortem.
  - Aditya: "no account manager layer" at the port; ran a post-mortem on a
    4-month lost deal and turned it into team practice.
  - Meghna: no escalation to management in 14 months; fixed a customs hold
    overnight before the client knew.
  - Rohan: works with forwarder ops "without a product layer"; comfortable
    as the most senior engineer in the room.
  - Sunita: owns processes end to end "with limited oversight".
Comparison group: Preetham is one of 12 engineers inside a 3,200-person
company. Vikram's CV is polished, success-only, and inside a 4-PM team with
senior PMs above. (Rahul is the exception here; he reports straight to a
CEO, so this pattern alone does not predict; combined with Pattern 1 it does.)

WHAT THE DATA SAYS NOT TO REWARD
  - Credentials and pedigree: Vikram has the XLRI MBA, Product School CPO,
    Reforge, a conference talk. He is the closest match to a typical PM
    job spec, and rated Meets.
  - Big-company brand and scale metrics: Preetham has clean scale
    metrics (2M+ events/day, 99.95% uptime) and rated Below.
  These get ZERO weight in the rubric.

CAVEATS
  - Eight hires is a small sample; treat weights as a starting hypothesis
    and re-calibrate after the next 3-5 hires.
  - Profiles used here are the hires' CVs; interview notes were not
    available, so patterns are drawn from CV evidence only.
  - Only 2 of the 8 were PMs. The rubric generalises from all functions
    because the pattern holds across functions.

======================================================================
PART 3: SCORING SCALE (used for every criterion, both roles)
======================================================================

5 = Strong, specific evidence, repeated across more than one role/job
4 = Clear, specific evidence in at least one role
3 = Partial or indirect evidence (adjacent context, or claimed but thin)
2 = Weak: generic statements only, no concrete example
1 = No evidence in the CV

Rules:
  - Score ONLY on evidence written in the CV. No evidence = 1. Do not infer.
  - Every score must cite the CV line(s) that justify it.
  - Weighted score = sum over criteria of (weight x score / 5). Range 20-100.
  - Ignore: name, gender, age, photo, college name/tier, employment gaps,
    certifications, company brand. These carry zero weight.

PART 4: PRODUCT MANAGER (PM) RUBRIC
======================================================================

PM-1  Hands-on logistics operations experience                     30%
      Did they personally do freight / logistics / supply chain
      operations work (documentation, customs, carrier coordination,
      dispatch, exceptions, port/terminal work) before or alongside
      their product work?
      5: 2+ years doing ops work directly (e.g. Rohan, Sunita, Lavanya)
      3: Worked closely with ops teams but never did the job
         (e.g. Preetham's 3PL API integrations)
      1: No logistics exposure (e.g. Vikram, Rahul)
      Behind it: Pattern 1 (Rohan, Sunita, Aditya, Meghna, Lavanya).

PM-2  Builds from observed pain; peers adopt it                   20%
      Did they spot a problem in their own daily work and build a tool
      or process for it without being asked, which others then used?
      5: Multiple self-started builds with named adoption
         (Rohan's tracker -> 12-person team; Lavanya's dashboard ->
         2 other teams)
      3: One example, or built only for their own use
      1: Only assigned work described
      Behind it: Pattern 2 (Rohan, Sunita, Lavanya, Meghna).

PM-3  Sole ownership with no layer in between                      20%
      Have they owned an area where they were the final point of
      accountability: sole PM, no manager/account layer, founder-facing?
      5: Sole owner of a product/area with outcomes attributed to them
         (Lavanya: sole PM, three areas)
      3: Owned features inside a larger PM team with seniors above
         (Vikram)
      1: Contributor role only
      Behind it: Pattern 3 (Lavanya, Aditya, Meghna, Rohan, Sunita).

PM-4  Discovery directly with operators                            15%
      Do they go to the frontline user (forwarder ops staff, carriers,
      dispatchers) and change the product because of what they found?
      5: Named discovery with ops users leading to a measurable change
         (Lavanya's slot-booking finding -> 60% fewer tickets)
      3: User research with non-operator buyers / admins
      1: No evidence of talking to users
      Behind it: Lavanya, Rohan (works with forwarder ops teams during
      rollouts), Aditya (alongside terminal ops at peak).

PM-5  Candour: kills, post-mortems, owning what went wrong         15%
      Does the CV show them stopping their own work on evidence, or
      naming a failure and what they changed?
      5: Explicit kill / loss / outage with the lesson and follow-up
         (Lavanya killed 2 features; Aditya's lost-deal post-mortem)
      3: Mentions a problem they fixed, but not their own miss
      1: Success-only narrative
      Behind it: Pattern 3 (Lavanya, Aditya, Meghna); absent in Vikram
      and Rahul.

TOTAL                                                              100%

======================================================================
PART 5: SENIOR PRODUCT MANAGER (SPM) RUBRIC
======================================================================
Context: the SPM reports directly to Arjun with no Head of Product above.
They will be the senior-most product person and will set practice for the
PM who joins after. Weight shifts from "doer" signals to "owner and
system-builder" signals, while keeping the ops-first signal heavy.

SPM-1 Hands-on logistics operations experience                     25%
      Same definition and anchors as PM-1.
      5 for SPM also expects the ops experience to have shaped decisions
      they later made in their function (Rohan turning CHA work into the
      BoL module).
      Behind it: Pattern 1 (all 5 high-rated hires).

SPM-2 Senior-most owner, no one above them in the function         25%
      Have they been the most senior person accountable for an area,
      making calls without a layer above to escalate to?
      5: Led a function or platform as senior-most, decisions attributed
         to them (Rohan: lead engineer, "most senior in the room";
         Sunita: independent consultant owning client migrations end
         to end)
      3: Sole owner of an area but with a senior manager above
      1: Team member only
      Behind it: Pattern 3 (Rohan, Sunita, Aditya).

SPM-3 Turns fixes into standing practice                            20%
      Beyond building a tool: did they install a process that the team
      now runs without them?
      5: Named practice still in use (Rohan's code review + on-call ->
         40% fewer P1s; Aditya's post-mortem now standard qualification;
         Sunita's SOPs adopted in full; Meghna's onboarding framework)
      3: Built something adopted, but tied to their own presence
      1: No evidence
      Behind it: Pattern 2 at senior level (Rohan, Aditya, Sunita,
      Meghna).

SPM-4 Judgement under pressure, stated plainly                      15%
      Evidence of hard calls made fast with incomplete information, and
      honest accounts of what went wrong.
      5: Kill decisions, time-pressured migrations, or losses named
         with the reasoning (Lavanya's kills; Rohan's vendor migration
         under time pressure; Aditya's loss post-mortem)
      3: Handled pressure, but no decision or failure described
      1: Success-only narrative
      Behind it: Pattern 3 (Lavanya, Rohan, Aditya).

SPM-5 Grows people who then get promoted                            15%
      Have they trained or mentored someone whose progress is named?
      5: Mentee promoted, with timeline (Rohan: mentee to mid-level in
         14 months; Sunita: trainee to team lead in 18 months)
      3: Mentored or ran 1:1s, no outcome named
      1: No evidence
      Behind it: Rohan, Sunita. (Weaker signal: Preetham also mentored,
      without a named outcome, so this is weighted lower.)

TOTAL                                                              100%

======================================================================
PART 6: HOW THE SCORE IS USED
======================================================================

Every candidate is scored against BOTH rubrics regardless of the role
they applied for (a PM applicant may be a stronger SPM fit, and the
reverse).

Bands (weighted score out of 100):
  75-100  Shortlist: top of dashboard, gets brief + draft invite
  55-74   Review: Arjun reads the brief before deciding
  20-54   Below the line: draft rejection, Arjun still reviews once

The system never rejects anyone. It ranks and explains; Arjun confirms
every email before it is sent. (Checks 06 and 09.)
